Sky Map · legibility mockups
PROPOSED · companion to docs/design/sky-legibility.md

A sky map that shows the sky you can actually see

These mockups use the real v2 catalogue: 5,998 stars to magnitude 6.2, and the IAU stick figures. They're drawn in a canvas to show the proposed behaviour, not as pixel-exact renderer output. Star colours for most stars, the Milky Way shape, constellation boundaries and the Cygnus art are illustrative stand-ins, as each caption notes.

§2 · HOW STARS LOOK

A continuous ramp and a chart halo

Both skies show every star in the catalogue, with today's constellation lines. The difference is how stars are drawn (the proposed sky also uses the new label styles from §8). Proposed stars shrink smoothly with magnitude, carry a background-coloured ring so lines stop short of them, and (optionally) show colour on bright stars only.

Proposed options

Field of view (both)75°

Sizes are in dp and stay the same at every zoom (D12). Zoom changes how many stars fit, not how big they are.

Colours: Betelgeuse, Rigel, Aldebaran and other named bright stars use real B−V values. The rest are random stand-ins until the Yale Bright Star Catalogue join is done.

§1 · SKY CONDITIONS

"How dark is your sky?"

One row at the top of the Layers sheet opens this half-height sheet. The map stays visible and updates live. Changing a preset animates the limit over 600 ms, so faint stars drain away or fill in. Try City, then Dark. Then move the Sun below the horizon and watch stars come out one by one.

§4–5 · CONSTELLATION LINES AND BOUNDARIES

Core shapes stay strong. The rest follows the stars.

Each figure's recognisable shape (the Big Dipper inside Ursa Major) is drawn heavier. The extended figure is thinner, appears as you zoom in, and a segment fades when either of its stars is below the limit. In a city, Ursa Major reduces to the Big Dipper without any extra rule.

Sky (proposed)

Field of view (both)60°

Boundaries are dashed and neutral, so they stay distinct from figures in night mode. Selecting a constellation draws its own boundary solid, with a faint fill, even when the layer is off.

Core shapes are hand-tagged here for UMa, UMi, Ori, Cyg, Leo, Peg and Gem. Elsewhere, segments with both stars brighter than mag 3 stand in. The UMa boundary is an approximate stepped outline for style only. The real data is CDS VI/49, with constant-declination edges drawn as small circles.

§6–7 · THE MILKY WAY AND CONSTELLATION ART

Pictures that know when to step back

The Milky Way is a stylised isophote band in three soft levels, the way printed atlases draw it. Its opacity follows the sky limit, so city users don't get a glowing band they can't see. Searching "Milky Way" points at its highest visible part. Constellation art is monochrome line art, shown for the focused constellation only, and it hands over to lines as you zoom in.

Sky

Field of view95°

The art fades out between 55° and 25° field of view, so zooming in always ends on lines and stars. Night mode caps both the art and the band at half opacity.

The band here is procedural. The real one would be generated from Tycho-2 star counts, so the Great Rift comes from the data. The swan is a rough sketch where cleaned Bode Uranographia art would go, registered on Deneb, Albireo and the wing stars.

§8 · LABELS AND TRANSITIONS

How things appear and disappear

The proposed skies above use three label styles: constellations in spaced small caps, stars in regular weight, and planets in their own colour. Labels have a density budget of about one per 2 cm², try four positions before giving up, and never label a star the sky setting has hidden.

What drives the changeTransitionWhy
A continuous value: twilight, Moon, the sky limitFade by value over a 0.5-mag soft edgeStars come out one by one, as they do outside. No timers and no lag.
Zoom: extended lines, art hand-over, label thresholdFade by field of view, tied to the pinchReversible mid-gesture. Nothing catches up after you stop.
Pan and focus: the centred constellation, art dwellTime-based, 400–600 ms, with hysteresisPrevents flicker when sweeping across a boundary.
User changes a toggle or preset~200 ms fade, or the limit animates over ~600 msInstant reads as a glitch. Slow reads as lag.
Off-screenHard cullNothing to see, so nothing to fade.

Night mode check: try the toggle at the top. Hierarchy is carried by size, luminance, width and dash pattern, so nothing above depends on hue.
