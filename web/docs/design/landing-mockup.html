How it works

Your phone already knows
which way it's pointing.

Sky Map reads your compass, accelerometer and location, works out exactly where you're aimed, and draws the sky onto your screen. Move the phone and the chart moves with you. No internet needed — the whole catalogue ships with the app.

COPY NOTE: deliberately avoids "augmented reality". Camera mode is behind an experiment flag, so nothing here promises a camera view yet. When it ships, this paragraph and the "Camera mode" row below are the only two places to edit.

Search and point

Type any object and follow the arrow until the target locks on. Works for planets, Messier objects, and 100,000+ stars.

Layers you control

Constellations, planets, deep-sky objects, meteor showers, the celestial grid — switch any of them on or off from the rail.

Time travel

Wind the sky forward or back. Check where Jupiter will be on Saturday, or what the sky looked like the night you were born.

Tap for detail

Magnitude, distance, rise and set times, and the mythology — one tap on anything in view.

Night mode

The whole interface shifts to deep red so you keep your dark adaptation while you're actually outside.

Your horizon

Sky Map knows the terrain around you, so it can tell you what's genuinely visible and what's behind a hill.

Version 2

Rebuilt, not restyled.

The original shipped on the first Android phone in 2009. Version 2 keeps everything people stayed for and replaces everything underneath.

  • New interface
    Built on modern Android

    A complete rewrite of the UI layer, so the app finally looks like it belongs on the phone you're holding.

  • New
    Terrain-aware horizon

    Sky Map now accounts for the landscape around you, not just a flat horizon line.

  • New
    Redesigned object cards

    More data, better typography, and a layout that works one-handed in the dark.

  • Improved
    Sensor smoothing

    Less jitter, faster settling, and clearer guidance when your compass genuinely needs help.

  • Coming later
    Camera mode

    Line the chart up against the real sky through your camera. In testing now — we'll announce it when it's ready.

Camera mode sits last and is labelled "coming later" in Lens Blue, not gold — so it reads as a roadmap item rather than something you can open today. If it's not ready at launch, delete the row; nothing else on the page depends on it.

Where this came from

Sixteen years, three owners,
one app.

2009

A 20% project at Google

Built by a small team in Pittsburgh and launched on the very first Android phone at Searchology.

2012

Open sourced

Google stopped active development and released the code. The original engineers kept building it on their own time, with Google's blessing and without Google's resources.

Today

Still the same people

Maintained by two of the original developers, still in Pittsburgh, still free.
